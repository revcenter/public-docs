Write your request the way you would brief a sourcer: the role, where it is, and what matters. Revcenter reads
it and shows you the plan before anything starts. Change anything in the plan, then start the search. The same
request always gives the same plan.

## Must match, Ranks higher, Left out

Every rule in the plan is tagged with how it counts:

| Tag | What happens | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Must match** | Only people who match are searched. | What the job can't do without: the title, the place, a licence, a level |
| **Ranks higher** | Everyone is searched; people who match come first. Nobody is removed for missing it. | Nice to have: a preferred employer, a skill, a range of years |
| **Left out** | People who match are removed. | Titles, employers or places you don't want |

**Suggested** marks something Revcenter added that your request didn't say, such as a level it read from the
title. Keep it, change it, or remove it; once you edit it, it's yours and the tag goes.

Must match narrows who is searched, so a good Must match saves credits. Ranks higher only moves people up or down
the list.

## Where the role is

The place is the one rule every search has. Shortlists are people who can commute to the job: the job's city
and the towns around it, about 25 to 40 miles. Say the city ("Lancaster, PA"), and a distance if you want a
different one ("within 30 miles"). For a remote role, say it's remote. "Open to relocating" doesn't widen the
search, because a profile doesn't say it.

## What you can ask for

| What | Say it like this | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Titles | "Titles: Construction Project Manager, Data Center PM, Site PM" | Name 3 to 10 titles people really hold. If you name none, Revcenter uses the role's title and its closest synonyms. A bare title like "Project Manager" isn't searched on its own next to a specific one: it would match every project manager anywhere. |
| Title rule | `("Project Manager" OR "Construction Manager") AND "Data Center" NOT Assistant` | Type it on its own line or after "Titles:" and it is kept exactly as written. AND, OR, NOT in capitals, brackets and "quoted phrases". Anyone whose current title doesn't fit is dropped before their profile is read. |
| Profile keywords | `Keywords: "data center" AND (construction OR "mission critical")` | Searches the whole profile, not only titles, so it reaches people whose titles don't name the specialty. Use OR, not commas. |
| Left-out titles | "Not assistant PMs or project coordinators" | People whose current title matches are left out. |
| Past titles | "Previously a superintendent" | People who held the title before their current job. |
| Level | "Manager level", "director or VP" | Searched with one level either side. Often Suggested from the title. |
| Employers | "Currently at Turner, Clayco or AECOM", "ex-McKinsey", "not from Clayco" | Name the firms. "Only at" or "currently at" is Must match; "ideally from" is Ranks higher; "not from" is Left out. "Ex-" or "formerly at" means they worked there before. |
| Employer size | "Companies with 200 to 5,000 employees" | Sizes come in bands, so the edges are approximate. |
| Current employer's industry | "Currently in construction" | The industry of the company they work for now. |
| Job function | "Project management function", "not sales" | |
| Years of experience | "5 to 10 years" | Must match narrows the search, with a little tolerance at each end; Ranks higher costs points instead. |
| Time in the current job | "At least 2 years in their current role", "changed jobs in the last 90 days" | |
| Requirements | "Must have CPA", "PE license" | Keep each to a short term a profile would contain. A sentence is kept as context for scoring, not matched word for word. |
| Skills | "Skills: scheduling, MEP coordination, Procore" | Ranks people whose profiles show them. |
| Schools | "Went to Purdue or UIUC" | |
| Profile language | "Profile in Spanish" | The language the profile is written in. For a language someone speaks, say "speaks Spanish". |
| Cut-off | "Shortlist anyone 70 or above" | Say it once results are in. From 50 to 85; the default is 80. |
| Credit cap | "Use up to 2,500 credits" | The most this search may use. 3 credits per profile delivered. People you already have are free. See [Pricing and credits](/pricing). |

## Ways to search

| Approach | Example | Use it when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Titles in a place | "Construction Project Managers in Chicago, 5 to 10 years" | The title says what the job is. The default, and it finds the most people. |
| A title rule in a place | `"Project Manager" AND ("Data Center" OR "Mission Critical")` in Chicago | The specialty is in people's titles. Precise: one such search found 24 people, 23 of them right, where a plain title list matched about 14,000. |
| Profile keywords | Project Manager, with `Keywords: "data center"` | The specialty is in people's experience, not their titles. Combine with a title rule for niche roles. |
| Titles at named employers | "Engagement managers in Boston, only McKinsey, BCG and Bain" | The client wants people from specific firms. The plan shows which company each name matched; a name that matches none is shown as not found. |
| Everyone at named firms | "Everyone at Bain & Company in Boston, director level" | You want a team, not one role. Add a level or function to keep it focused. |

## Tips that improve results

- **Name the titles yourself.** Three to ten real titles beat one vague one.
- **Give years as a range.** "5 to 10 years" ranks better than "senior".
- **Name employers, not descriptions.** "Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG" is matched to the firms; "top firms" can only be guessed.
- **Keep requirements short.** "PMP", "data center construction", "Procore". Put the story in the description.
- **Name the words to leave out.** NOT removes titles that contain those words: for an Operations Manager,
  `NOT (Revenue OR Marketing OR IT OR Legal)`.
- **Read the plan before you start.** Check every Suggested rule and every Must match.

Developers: in criteria, Must match is `flag: "must"` (or `enforcement: "hard"` on years), Ranks higher is
`flag: "prefer"` (or `enforcement: "preferred"`), and Left out is `flag: "exclude"` or an `exclude_*` field. See
the [Criteria reference](/guides/criteria).
